## Prior novelty audit already warns against nearby relabeling

`literature-novelty-audit-20261121/literature_review.md` compares eighteen primary-source works. It explicitly rejects treating selective test-time augmentation plus a finite-sample accept/decline decision as sufficient novelty. It includes group-theoretic augmentation, selective view choice, adaptive pass counts, learned aggregation, conformal/risk control, imperfect invariance, label-efficient risk correction, and unlabeled identifiability.

Its provisional “sharp resource-aware certifiability boundary under imperfect symmetry” was not a validated contribution. The later joint-asymmetry audit rejects the particular proposed interaction formula as standard TV common-mass algebra. Therefore returning to generic symmetry certification or unidentified risk control would repeat an already crowded branch. A new minimax theorem would need a genuinely different target and a separation from those results, not just a new empirical bound.

## Quantitative limits that matter for selecting new work

- Fixed-half-turn symmetric selection is nearly at its measured oracle frontier. The meaningful remaining error is mostly model/label/calibration quality, not a richer score table.
- GPU deployment did not reward sparse second-view selection on the tested implementations; larger model computation alone did not solve routing overhead. Actual CPU gains at larger widths were separately measured.
- Real-image TTA depends on the training transformation; a favorable artificial pair-closed distribution does not establish original-population benefit.
- The latest full-budget training-allocation comparison retains the existing 2304-training recipe. No alternative was independently promoted.
- The old geometry observations remain descriptive; the full tangent control did not clear its declared predictive screen.

## Archive-untested directions that are genuinely different in scope

These are leads for **original-source review**, not recommended contributions or claims of novelty.

1. **Stochastic inverse-metric/gradient coupling.** No dedicated minibatch representation-PB study was found. If the same random minibatch supplies both the inverse metric and the gradient, its expected update differs from one using an independent geometry batch, even when the individual gradient estimate is unbiased. This is different from the already completed deterministic full-batch label-replica calculation at initialization. The danger is immediate reduction to ordinary inverse-estimator bias or stochastic Newton/KFAC literature. A worthwhile question must distinguish a new task-dependent effect from that known baseline; merely showing the two expectations differ is insufficient.

2. **Geometry sampling/rank and approximate solves.** No dedicated sketch, leverage-sampled geometry, coreset or low-rank metric study was located. Periodic caching is already tried and must remain excluded. This area is likely occupied by randomized Newton, subsampled natural gradient and matrix approximation results; a spectral-norm error bound alone is not a contribution. The train-to-population predictive consequence under a carefully controlled geometry-sampling law would need independent justification.

3. **Asymptotic margin/implicit bias of the exact residual-pullback flow.** No dedicated asymptotic study was located. The two-block bounded tanh features with trainable residual-output matrices are not globally homogeneous, so importing a homogeneous-network max-margin result would be unsafe. This is potentially mathematically distinct from the finite-progress feedback claim, but original natural-gradient/implicit-bias papers must be checked before any calculation is promoted. A fixed-tangent max-margin derivation alone would be ordinary transformed-feature analysis.

4. **Coordinate/damping sensitivity.** No substantial reparameterization experiment was found beyond caveats and the recent fixed-preconditioner reductions. Generic Euclidean-damping coordinate dependence is already known and likely too routine to pursue as a main candidate. It belongs on the exclusion test list unless a precise nonstandard prediction survives.

5. **Continual-task preservation or representation memory.** No dedicated study was located, but projected gradients, natural-gradient trust regions and Fisher-based continual learning are obvious prior threats. This would also expand the task beyond the existing prediction/generalization question; it should not be adopted solely because it is untested locally.

Among these, stochastic inverse-metric/gradient coupling is the clearest *different experiment class* for immediate original-source screening. Its novelty burden is high. This inventory does not recommend enlarging the old failed geometry study or silently changing its criterion.
