# Finite damping: exact endpoint neutrality from central invariants

Date: 2026-11-21. This audit follows the independent review of the two-condition first-order classification. No broad numerical search or additional damped trajectory was run by this agent. The algebraic bridge was independently checked against the algebra agent's proof; the root agent owns the separate composite-block validation.

## Outcome

The proposed boundary between first-order neutrality and possible higher-order damping sensitivity disappears for the spaces satisfying the two closure conditions. Their generated Jordan algebra has only central directions normal to the measurement space. This yields exact conserved quantities for every nonnegative damping value and identifies every regular interpolating endpoint with the same strictly convex Bregman projection.

Thus there is no passing-space second-order-damping counterexample among convergent SPD flows in this model. The qualification is about existence and convergence, not about an unproved cancellation at higher order. Small-target convergence follows from the existing compact-SPD argument for each bounded damping interval and compact positive-definite weight family. Global convergence for arbitrary targets and weights is not proved here.

The conservation/KKT mechanism is standard once the algebraic structure is known. It should not be promoted as a novel general principle of optimization. The potentially distinct statement is the classification of exactly which measurement spaces acquire this structure under the specified inverse-Lyapunov correction, subject to the separate primary-source audit.

## Algebraic premise, now proved rather than assumed

Let `S subset Sym(n)` satisfy, for all `Z,U in S`,

\[
[Z,[Z,U]]\in S,\qquad
\{Z,[Z,[Z,U]]\}\in S.
\]

Let `J=Jordan(S,I)` be the unital real Jordan subalgebra generated by `S` in symmetric matrices. The completed algebraic argument shows

\[
N_J:=J\cap S^\perp\ \subseteq\ \operatorname{center}(J).
\]

Here centrality means matrix commutation with every element of `J`. The proof does not assume that `S` itself is Jordan closed. The independent review checked the following bridge:

1. The positivity lemma in `REFEREE_AUDIT.md` makes every degree-two normal defect `P_N(Z circ U)` central in `J`.
2. With `k=[S,S]`, Lie-triple closure makes `S` a finite-dimensional orthogonal `k`-module. Split it into nontrivial irreducible modules `S_i` and its fixed subspace `S_c`. Different modules commute as matrices, by the commutator norm identity; `S_c` is central.
3. Equivariance and the central-defect property give `S_i circ S_j=0` for `i!=j`, and

   `Z circ U = m_i(Z,U) + <Z,U> a_i`, with `m_i(Z,U) in S_i` and central `a_i` orthogonal to `S_i`.

4. For unit `Z` orthogonal to `U` in `S_i`, substitute this decomposition in

   `ad_Z^2(U)=4[Z^2 circ U-Z circ(Z circ U)]`.

   The central coefficients cancel because `<m_i(Z,Z),U>=<Z,m_i(Z,U)>`. Hence multiplication by `a_i` preserves `S_i`. It is self-adjoint and equivariant, so it is scalar there: `a_i U=alpha_i U`. Frobenius associativity gives `alpha_i=||a_i||_F^2>0`. The strict inequality follows because `S_i` is traceless while `tr(Z^2)>0` for nonzero `Z`, so `a_i` cannot vanish.

5. Multiplying `Z^2=m_i(Z,Z)+a_i` by `a_i` yields `a_i^2=alpha_i a_i`. Therefore `e_i=a_i/alpha_i` is a central idempotent, and `J_i=R e_i plus S_i` is a simple Jordan algebra. Distinct idempotents have orthogonal supports. A central generator in `S_c` acts by a scalar on each `S_i`, hence on each `e_i`; its remaining support generates a commutative algebra.

Consequently `J` is the direct sum of these simple factors and a commutative central algebra. The space `S` contains the traceless part of every non-scalar simple factor and an arbitrary subspace of the center. Its orthogonal complement inside `J` is therefore central. The algebra agent's report supplies the detailed decomposition; this audit independently verified the critical positivity, scalar-multiplication, and idempotent steps.

## Invariance of the generated algebra

Write `K_X(U)=XU+UX`, `T_X=K_X^-1`, and `M_lambda(X)=(I+lambda T_X)^-1`. For `X in J intersect SPD`, the Jordan property gives `K_X(J) subset J`. Since `K_X` is positive definite and self-adjoint, its inverse and the mobility also preserve `J`.

The initial state `sI` belongs to `J`, and every loss gradient belongs to `S subset J`. The product flow

\[
\dot X=-M_\lambda(X)G(X)
\]

therefore stays in `J` as long as it stays SPD. Components orthogonal to `J` cannot appear. This point is necessary: centrality is needed only for `N_J`, not for the entire ambient space `S^perp`.

## Exact conserved coordinates

For each `N in N_J`, define

\[
F_N(X)=\left\langle N,\ X+\frac\lambda2\log X\right\rangle.
\]

Centrality implies `[N,X]=0`, and hence

\[
T_X(N)=\tfrac12X^{-1}N,
\qquad D\log_X(N)=X^{-1}N.
\]

The operators are self-adjoint. Thus, along the actual flow,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}F_N(X)
=\langle N,(I+\lambda T_X)\dot X\rangle
=-\langle N,G(X)\rangle=0.
\]

These identities are exact for every fixed `lambda>=0`. They hold for any differentiable loss whose gradient lies in `S`, not only for a quadratic loss or diagonal loss weights. There is no claim that the entire metric is the Hessian of a potential: only the normal conserved coordinates are used.

The same sufficiency argument works for any SPD initialization `X0 in J`, with the corresponding initial invariant values. This is not a classification for arbitrary nonisotropic initializations outside `J`; the necessity proof of the two-condition criterion used isotropic initialization.

## Endpoint uniqueness and the implicit regularizer

Define the strictly convex potential

\[
\Phi_\lambda(X)=\tfrac12\|X\|_F^2
+\frac\lambda2\operatorname{tr}(X\log X-X),
\qquad
\nabla\Phi_\lambda(X)=X+\frac\lambda2\log X.
\]

Its Hessian is `I+(lambda/2)Dlog_X`, which is positive definite on SPD. Suppose two SPD limits `X1,X2 in J` interpolate the same measurements and share the conserved coordinates. Their difference `D=X1-X2` lies in `N_J`, while conservation gives

\[
\langle D,\nabla\Phi_\lambda(X_1)-\nabla\Phi_\lambda(X_2)\rangle=0.
\]

Strict monotonicity of the gradient makes this impossible unless `D=0`. Therefore all regular interpolating limits coincide, independently of fixed positive loss weights, optimization time parametrization, or other choices of a loss with the same constraints and gradient span.

There is also an ambient KKT characterization. A unital matrix Jordan algebra is closed under one-variable spectral functional calculus, so `log X` and `nabla Phi_lambda(X)` belong to `J`. The invariant equations imply

\[
\nabla\Phi_\lambda(X_\infty)-\nabla\Phi_\lambda(X_0)
\in J\cap N_J^\perp=S.
\]

Since `S=range(A*)`, this is the KKT condition for

\[
\boxed{\quad
X_\infty=\operatorname*{argmin}_{X\succ0:\,\mathcal AX=y}
D_{\Phi_\lambda}(X,X_0).
\quad}
\]

Strict convexity makes any such SPD solution the unique minimizer even in the ambient SPD space, not just in `J`. For `X0=sI`, the objective is explicitly

\[
D_{\Phi_\lambda}(X,sI)
=\tfrac12\|X-sI\|_F^2
+\frac\lambda2\operatorname{tr}\left[X(\log X-(\log s)I)-X+sI\right].
\]

This conclusion identifies an interpolating limit if one exists. It does not silently supply a global convergence proof for every feasible target. In the small-target regime already justified by the compact-SPD continuation argument, existence, convergence, and endpoint neutrality all hold without an extra convergence assumption, for each fixed bounded damping interval and compact SPD weight family.

## Why the first-order formula is recovered

Differentiate the KKT normal invariant at `lambda=0`, where `X*=sI+Delta` is the Euclidean interpolant and the response lies in `N_J`. One obtains

\[
C=-\tfrac12P_{N_J}[\log X_*-(\log s)I].
\]

Because the logarithm belongs to `J`, projection onto `N_J` agrees here with ambient projection onto `S^perp`. This is exactly the earlier first-order formula. The new structural result shows that this agreement is the derivative of an exact finite-damping conserved-coordinate law, rather than an isolated perturbative cancellation.

If the closure conditions fail, the earlier cubic or quartic fixed-weight construction still supplies arbitrarily small targets with a nonzero first damping-response contrast. Thus the passing/failing distinction is not erased by the finite-damping extension; its mathematical interpretation becomes a partial-potential conservation classification.

## Strict prior-work interpretation

[Marcotte, Gribonval, and Peyre, NeurIPS 2023](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/c7bee9b76be21146fd592fc2b46614d5-Paper-Conference.pdf) already frame conservation laws as a weaker requirement than a full mirror representation and explain how enough laws determine implicit bias. Their [ICML 2024 extension](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12888v1) covers non-Euclidean gradient flows. The conserved-coordinate calculation and the KKT implication above are instances of this existing strategy.

A newer close source, [Zhang and Peyre, October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09356), Appendix D, derives complete covariance-level conservation laws for split Bures flows using generated Lie algebras and orbit invariants. Its mobility is `K_X`, while the present damped full-representation mobility is `(I+lambda K_X^-1)^-1`; its split architecture and invariants also differ. It is therefore not an exact theorem match, but it further rules out claiming generic Bures conservation or Lie-algebra completeness as new.

The center-plus-traceless decomposition belongs to classical Euclidean Jordan algebra representation theory once the generator-to-algebra bridge is established. The bridge and the exact optimization equivalence must be assessed against that literature. A broader “new geometry explains feature learning” claim is not supported by this result.
