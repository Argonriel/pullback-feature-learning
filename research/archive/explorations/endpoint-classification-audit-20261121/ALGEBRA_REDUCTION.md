# Algebraic reduction of the two-condition criterion

November 21, 2026. This is an adversarial audit of the proposed classification, not another numerical screen.

**Verdict:** the class selected by the two polynomial conditions is classical. In the finite-dimensional real symmetric setting, those conditions are equivalent to saying that S is a **Lie triple ideal, also called an associator ideal**, of the unital Jordan algebra generated by S. Equivalently, S is invariant under all derivations of that generated algebra. Its noncentral part is the full traceless part of each non-scalar simple Jordan factor; the remaining freedom is an arbitrary subspace of the center.

The generator-to-ideal implication is proved below rather than assumed. This substantially reduces the novelty claim: there is no new family of algebraic structures here. A possible residual contribution is the particular low-degree membership test and its optimizer interpretation, but the reviewed sources do not establish priority for that formulation. The result should not be presented as a new structural classification without further evidence.

## 1. Original literature and terminology

[Brešar, Cabrera, Fošner, and Villena, 2005, *Lie triple ideals and Lie triple epimorphisms on Jordan and Jordan–Banach algebras*](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/90340), pages 209 and 213, define a Lie triple ideal M by `[M,J,J] subset M`, where the bracket is the Jordan associator. They identify the same objects as associator ideals, and note that the other placements also remain in M. Their Section 3 develops their structure; Lemma 3.3 gives the center/traceless/full alternatives for spin factors. This is an exact match of algebraic object once the bridge below is proved, rather than a merely suggestive name match.

[Jacobson, 1949, *Derivation Algebras and Multiplication Algebras of Semi-Simple Jordan Algebras*](https://www.math.uci.edu/~brusso/jacobsonAM1949.pdf), Theorem 2, proves that derivations of finite-dimensional semisimple Jordan algebras in characteristic zero are inner. Importantly, the generators in that statement range over the **whole** Jordan algebra. It does not by itself justify replacing them by elements of S; that was the gap requiring an argument.

[Benito, Draper, and Elduque, 1999, *On Some Algebras Related to Simple Lie Triple Systems*](https://investigacion.unirioja.es/documentos/5bbc680bb750603269e8017c/f/5ddc084152922232560fc774.pdf) study equivariant bilinear products on simple finite-dimensional Lie triple systems and their connection with simple Jordan algebras. This is a strong precedent for the irreducible-module/product part of the reduction below. Their stated problem is not the projected Lyapunov one-form or its two generator conditions; that distinction must be retained.

The Jordan product is commutative by definition. Accordingly, the decomposition below uses the term **non-scalar simple factor**, rather than the misleading phrase “noncommutative Jordan algebra.” Their matrix realizations can, of course, contain noncommuting matrices.

## 2. Precise statement

Let S be a real linear subspace of Sym(n), and let J be the smallest unital Jordan subalgebra of Sym(n) containing S, with

    x circ y = (xy+yx)/2.

Use the Frobenius inner product. Ordinary brackets below are matrix commutators. The following statements are equivalent:

1. For every z,u in S,

       [z,[z,u]] in S,                       (A)
       [z^2,[z,u]] in S.                     (CA)

2. S is a Lie triple ideal of J for its Jordan associator

       Assoc(x,y,z)=(x circ y) circ z-x circ(y circ z).

3. Every derivation of J leaves S invariant.
4. There is an orthogonal decomposition into Jordan ideals

       J = J_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum J_r direct-sum C_0,

   where the J_i are non-scalar simple Euclidean Jordan algebras and C_0 is commutative associative, such that

       S = (J_1)_0 direct-sum ... direct-sum (J_r)_0
           direct-sum S_c,

   with `(J_i)_0={x in J_i: tr x=0}` and `S_c subset Center(J)`.

The direct factors are factors of **the algebra generated by S and I**, not an arbitrarily chosen larger ambient Jordan algebra. The condition that J is generated by S rules out adding non-scalar factors on which S has no traceless component. The central subspace may couple the centers of different factors; it need not itself be a Jordan subalgebra.

The ambient dimension is finite, the matrices are real symmetric, and the positive Frobenius form is essential to this proof. No extension to arbitrary fields, indefinite trace forms, or general Jordan–Banach algebras is asserted.

## 3. From the generator conditions to a central quadratic defect

Let P be the Frobenius projection onto S and Q=Id-P. Set

    k=span{[x,y]:x,y in S}.

Polarization of (A), followed by Jacobi, gives `[k,S] subset S`. Jacobi also makes k a Lie algebra. Its elements are skew-symmetric, so their commutator actions on Sym(n) are skew-adjoint. Consequently Q commutes with every such action.

For z in S, put `b(z)=Q(z^2)`. This belongs to J because both z squared and P(z squared) belong to J. The self-adjoint positive operator `A_z=ad_z^2` preserves S and hence commutes with Q. Since `A_z(z^2)=0`,

    A_z b(z)=0,       so [z,b(z)]=0.

Condition (CA), and the fact that Q commutes with `ad_[z,u]`, give

    [[z,u],b(z)]=0                  for all z,u in S.

Differentiate `[z,b(z)]=0` in the direction u:

    [u,b(z)]=-[z,Q(zu+uz)].

Thus `[u,b(z)]` lies in the range of the self-adjoint operator ad_z on all real matrices. By Jacobi and the preceding identities it also lies in its kernel:

    [z,[u,b(z)]]=[[z,u],b(z)]+[u,[z,b(z)]]=0.

The range and kernel of a self-adjoint finite-dimensional operator are orthogonal. Therefore `[u,b(z)]=0`.

It follows that b(z) commutes with every generator in S, and hence with the full associative algebra generated by S and I. In particular it is central in J. Polarizing yields

    Q(x circ y) is central in J for all x,y in S,
    S circ S subset S+Center(J).                         (1)

This is the key strengthening supplied by (CA). Lie triple closure alone does not imply (1).

For the next steps one may define C directly as the matrices in J commuting with all generators. These matrices also commute with each other because they belong to the associative algebra generated by S. Thus C is a unital commutative associative algebra. The decomposition obtained below identifies it with the Jordan center, avoiding a hidden assumption about different notions of center.

## 4. An elementary irreducible decomposition

The action of k on S is skew-adjoint. Decompose S orthogonally into its fixed space S_c and nontrivial irreducible real modules S_i:

    S = S_c direct-sum S_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum S_r.

No classification of simple Lie algebras is needed. For x and y in two orthogonal k-invariant summands, the identity

    ||[x,y]||_F^2 = -<[[x,y],x],y>

shows that `[x,y]=0`: the double commutator on the right remains in the summand containing x. In particular distinct S_i commute as matrices.

Every c in S_c also commutes with all of S. Indeed `[c,y]` belongs to k, which fixes c, and the same norm identity gives zero. Hence S_c is central.

Let `k_i=[S_i,S_i]`. Cross-commutation shows that k_i acts only on S_i; it acts trivially on the other summands. Its action on S_i is nontrivial and irreducible. Otherwise the action of all k on that summand would be trivial. Also

    [k_i,S_i]=S_i.

Every S_i is orthogonal to C, since commutators are orthogonal to matrices commuting with k_i. In particular all elements of S_i have trace zero. This eliminates a possible ambiguity about diagonal copies of equivalent modules: the norm identity forces different invariant summands to commute, and their acting ideals have disjoint nontrivial actions.

## 5. Recovering the simple Jordan factors

The ordinary commutator action of k is a derivation of the Jordan product. Combining this equivariance with (1) gives

    S_i circ S_j = 0,                      i != j.        (2)

For example, k_i-equivariance forces the product to lie in S_i, because k_i acts trivially on all other modules and on C; k_j-equivariance forces it into S_j. Their intersection is zero.

For a single summand, the same argument gives

    x circ y = m_i(x,y)+<x,y>a_i,           x,y in S_i,   (3)

where `m_i(x,y) in S_i` and `a_i in C` is fixed. To justify the scalar form of the central term, apply any linear functional on C. The resulting symmetric bilinear form on S_i is k_i-invariant. Its representing self-adjoint operator commutes with k_i. Its eigenspaces are invariant, so irreducibility makes it scalar. This argument works over the real numbers without assuming that the entire commutant consists only of scalar operators.

The nontrivial real module S_i has dimension at least two. Fix u in S_i and choose a unit z in S_i orthogonal to u. Then

    z^2=m_i(z,z)+a_i,
    z circ u=m_i(z,u).

The exact special-Jordan identity is

    ad_z^2(u)=4[L_(z^2)-L_z^2]u,

where `L_x(y)=x circ y`; the first L uses the matrix z squared and the second is composition of operators. Hence

    a_i u = ad_z^2(u)/4
            -m_i(z,z) circ u + z circ m_i(z,u).          (4)

The central parts of the last two products cancel because the Frobenius form is associative for the Jordan product:

    <m_i(z,z),u>=<z,m_i(z,u)>.

All remaining terms in (4) belong to S_i. Thus a_i maps S_i into itself. Its multiplication is self-adjoint and commutes with k_i, so

    a_i u=alpha_i u               for every u in S_i.

Taking the inner product of a_i with z squared in (3) gives

    alpha_i=||a_i||_F^2>0.

Positivity is strict: m_i(z,z) has trace zero and a unit z has `tr(z^2)=1`, so `tr(a_i)=1` and a_i cannot vanish. Multiplying `z^2=m_i(z,z)+a_i` by a_i now gives

    a_i^2=alpha_i a_i.

Define `e_i=a_i/alpha_i`. Then e_i is a central symmetric idempotent, `e_i u=u` on S_i, and

    J_i=R e_i direct-sum S_i

is a unital Jordan subalgebra. Formula (2) implies `e_i e_j=0` for distinct i,j. In fact `alpha_i=1/tr(e_i)`, so the normalization is compatible with matrix representations of arbitrary multiplicity.

Each J_i is simple. A Jordan ideal in J_i is invariant under its inner derivations, including k_i. Since `R e_i` is a trivial one-dimensional module and S_i is an irreducible nontrivial module, the only possible invariant subspaces are 0, `R e_i`, S_i, and J_i. The middle two cannot be Jordan ideals: e_i times S_i is S_i, while a square of a nonzero element of S_i has a nonzero e_i component. Therefore S_i is exactly the traceless part `(J_i)_0`.

## 6. The center and the generated algebra

For c in S_c, multiplication by c maps every S_i into itself, by the same equivariance argument. It is self-adjoint and hence scalar there, say

    c u=chi_i(c)u,                u in S_i.

Using the expression for e_i in terms of a square and m_i shows `c e_i=chi_i(c)e_i`. Thus the residual matrices

    c_res=c-sum_i chi_i(c)e_i

are supported on `e_0=I-sum_i e_i`, commute with each other, and annihilate every J_i. Let C_0 be the commutative associative algebra generated by e_0 and those residuals, omitting the zero factor if e_0=0.

Every e_i belongs to the Jordan algebra generated by S, by its construction. Conversely the direct sum of the J_i and C_0 is a Jordan subalgebra containing S and I. Therefore it is exactly J. Its center consists of the scalar identities of the J_i together with C_0. This proves the asserted decomposition and also proves the generator-to-whole-algebra bridge that cannot be obtained just by citing inner derivations.

## 7. Equivalence with the classical ideal conditions

In a special Jordan algebra,

    4 Assoc(x,y,z)=[y,[x,z]].

Under the decomposition, a Jordan associator vanishes on central inputs and its component in each J_i has trace zero. Hence `Assoc(S,J,J) subset S`: S is a classical Lie triple/associator ideal.

Conversely, if S is such an ideal, then

    [z,[z,u]]=4 Assoc(z,z,u),
    [z^2,[z,u]]=4 Assoc(z,z^2,u)

belong to S, proving (A) and (CA) directly. Thus this is an exact equivalence, not a comparison only at the level of examples.

For derivations, every inner operator has the form

    [L_a,L_b](x)=(1/4)[[a,b],x],           a,b in J.

The decomposition makes S invariant under these operators, and Jacobson's theorem identifies all derivations with their span. Conversely invariance under all derivations immediately yields (A) and (CA) by applying `[L_u,L_z]` and `[L_u,L_(z^2)]` to z. This supplies both directions of the derivation-invariance formulation with the correct whole-J quantifiers.

These spaces are **not generally Jordan ideals or inner ideals**. An exact two-by-two example distinguishes the terms: take S to be all traceless symmetric 2-by-2 matrices, J=Sym(2), and z=diag(1,-1). The space passes (A) and (CA) and is an associator ideal. But `z circ z=I` is outside S, so it is not a Jordan ideal. The special-Jordan quadratic representation gives `U_z(I)=z I z=I`, also outside S, so it is not an inner ideal. This is a definitional counterexample, requiring no numerical experiment.

## 8. Adversarial proof checks

Several tempting shortcuts were not used:

- From `S circ S subset S+Center(J)` alone, one cannot simply announce that `S+Center(J)` is Jordan closed. Multiplication of a central element by S still needs control. Sections 4–6 establish that control through the module decomposition and equation (4).
- Real irreducibility does not make every commuting linear operator scalar. Only the **self-adjoint** commuting operators used here are forced to be scalar by their invariant eigenspaces.
- Fixed-spectrum or simple-eigenvalue assumptions are unnecessary. The proof includes forced multiplicities and spin-factor realizations.
- The ordinary Frobenius trace on a concrete representation may scale the normalized Jordan trace. The identity `a_i=e_i/tr(e_i)` accounts for that scale.
- Lie triple closure of S internally is weaker than being an ideal in J. The central-defect lemma and generated-algebra argument are the missing implications; neither is silently borrowed from a theorem about arbitrary elements of J.
- No conditional convergence result about optimization is used to prove this algebraic classification. Conversely, the algebra alone does not prove global convergence of every optimization trajectory.

The central-defect lemma and the recovery of the simple factors were independently cross-checked by other agents, including the sign and normalization in (4), positivity of alpha_i, orthogonality of the e_i, and the treatment of central residuals. No previous numerical grid was rerun. The report consists of exact identities and a finite-dimensional proof.

## 9. Consequence for the novelty claim

The earlier two-condition criterion does not identify a previously unknown structural class. It recognizes the classical associator ideals of the generated Euclidean Jordan algebra. Commuting spaces, full traceless components, block examples, and spin-factor examples are instances of this existing theory; the star-shaped Lie triple counterexample fails precisely because it is not an ideal in its generated Jordan algebra.

It would therefore be inaccurate to advertise the result as a new classification of matrix geometries or as a new noncommutative implicit-bias mechanism. The stricter, defensible residual question is whether the exact cubic/quartic **generator test**, together with its equivalence to the particular endpoint-weight question, is already stated or follows as a short recognized criterion in prior algebra or optimization work. The reviewed primary sources establish substantial overlap and the classical nature of the resulting objects, but do not yet certify literal priority for that test.

The derived structure also explains why a search for a passing space with higher-order finite-damping weight sensitivity is unlikely to succeed in this model: the unmeasured directions inside J are central, allowing separate conserved projected trace/logarithm quantities. The companion dynamics audit should state the precise selection and convergence qualifications. That stronger consequence further reduces the case for presenting first-order closure alone as a new phenomenon.
