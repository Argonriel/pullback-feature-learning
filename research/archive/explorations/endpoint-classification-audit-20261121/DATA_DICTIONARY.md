# Data, provenance, and validation

This round contains one new numerical case and four integrations. No old grid,
benchmark, or reserved cohort was rerun. The results are numerical consistency
checks, not evidence of mathematical priority.

## Reproducible files

- `finite_damping_validation/results.json`: all scalar settings and diagnostics.
- `finite_damping_validation/matrices.npz`: NumPy arrays containing the eight
  orthonormal measurement basis matrices, the two orthonormal central normal
  matrices, the measurement target, both loss weights, the predicted endpoint,
  and all four integrated endpoints.
- `source_snapshot/verify_finite_damping.py`: exact executable source.
- `source_snapshot/README.md`: usage instructions.
- `provenance.json`: source/data hashes and Python/NumPy environment versions.
- `manifest.json`: SHA-256 and size of each packaged content file.
- `ARCHIVE_CHECK.json`: verification of the package and the four prior archives.

The output directory is required to be new. No failed or exploratory numerical
attempt preceded the recorded four-run check in this round.

## Numerical conventions

The matrices are 6 by 6, with 2, 3, and 1 dimensional diagonal blocks.
The measurement basis contains the two traceless Sym(2) directions, five
traceless Sym(3) directions, and one center direction. Relative to the normalized
block identities I2/sqrt(2), I3/sqrt(3), I1, the latter has coefficients
proportional to (1,-0.7,0.4). The two central normal directions span its orthogonal
complement in the three-dimensional center. Singular-value decomposition chooses
their signs; signs have no effect on the constraints or invariant equations.

The reported target is the coordinate vector of the displacement from I6, not
the raw measurement of X. Damping is 1.7, initialization is I6, and all losses
have gradient given by the stated weight times the measurement residual.
The dense weight is generated by QR using NumPy seed 20261121, with eigenvalues
0.7 through 2.1 in increments of 0.2. The actual weight matrices are saved.

RK4 integrates to time 100 at steps 0.025 and 0.0125. Each derivative uses a
symmetric eigenvalue decomposition to apply the exact mobility factor
(x_i+x_j)/(x_i+x_j+lambda). The independent endpoint calculation uses Newton's
method in the two unobserved central coordinates and the conserved quantities
tr[N(X+(lambda/2)log X)]. Its Jacobian uses the central identity
Dlog_X[N]=X^-1 N, rather than trajectory integration.

All norms are Frobenius or Euclidean coordinate norms. The minimum eigenvalue
and maximum invariant drift are sampled every 40 steps and at the endpoint;
they are not certified continuous-time extrema. The measurement and prediction
errors are evaluated at the endpoint of each integration. The optimality
residual tests whether the potential gradient difference lies in S in the full
ambient Sym(6), not only inside the block-diagonal generated algebra.
