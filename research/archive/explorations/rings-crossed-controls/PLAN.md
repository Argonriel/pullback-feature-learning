# Crossing rate choice and training budget on concentric rings

This follow-up estimates the change in the validation gap between full-batch GD and 100 pullback updates followed by GD. The motivating studies changed both the rates and the budget and used distinct draws. All factor comparisons here use the same 16 new paired draws. Each pair has 256 training and 256 validation examples from the previous noisy concentric-rings generator; seed i=1,...,16 uses 91000+i for training, 92000+i for initialization and 93000+i for validation. No test data. The fixed readout, model, float64 precision, and losses match the previous rounds.

Two rate policies: use GD rate 0.125 throughout the GD phases of both methods, or use the rates previously selected on separate rings tuning pairs for the 100 ms objective (0.5 for GD, 2.0 for the GD phase of the hybrid). Full pullback uses rate 8 and damping 0.1 for its first 100 updates in the hybrid. No new rate selection. The selected policy at 500 steps is the direct transfer of those same values. This isolates changing the policy from changing the endpoint.

Two endpoints are taken from each trajectory: exactly 500 updates, and the last whole update within 100,000,000 ns of accumulated core-update wall time. Gradient/Jacobian computation, solve and parameter addition are timed. Exclude data setup, scoring, state copying and timing bookkeeping. Retain the first crossing time. The 500-step snapshot may precede or follow the time endpoint. Score both saved states after training ends.

Run each of the four method/rate combinations three times per pair, sequentially on one BLAS thread. Use a fixed schedule with balanced run positions for all four combinations in each repeat, shuffle pair blocks, and warm up both optimizers first using separate inputs. Do not replace slow or unfavorable runs. Record any numerical failure and do not summarize incomplete pairs as full coverage.

Primary outcome is validation logistic loss. Also report validation accuracy in percentage points, updates and core time. First average the three timing repeats within each seed. Define the hybrid advantage as GD loss minus hybrid loss, and hybrid accuracy minus GD accuracy. Compare budgets at each fixed policy and policies at each fixed budget. Report the interaction (the rate-policy effect at 100 ms minus that at 500 steps), seed-level variation, and paired bootstrap intervals over full seed blocks. The setting is one small synthetic task on this CPU. The prespecified design uses the entire completed grid regardless of ranking.
