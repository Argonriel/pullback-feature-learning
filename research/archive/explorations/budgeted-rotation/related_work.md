# Related work and the contribution of this study

**Optimization geometry.** Nurbekyan, Lei, and Yang formulate natural-gradient updates for state-space metrics as least-squares problems. Their discrete L² construction is a direct precedent for fitting a parameter step to a desired output velocity through a Jacobian. Our optimizer uses the Euclidean geometry of a two-coordinate network representation and adds parameter damping. Agarwal et al.'s AdaGraft separates the parameter-space length of an optimizer update from its direction. Our earlier comparisons transport that experimental idea to the predicted motion of the training features or the readout logit. [Nurbekyan, Lei, and Yang, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1477805) · [Agarwal et al., 2020, Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.11803)

**Symmetry and prediction disagreement.** Lyle et al. prove that averaging predictions over a distribution-preserving group can reduce population risk under a convex loss. Wood et al. give an exact diversity decomposition for log loss with a normalized geometric mean of predictive probabilities, equivalent to averaging logits. For two Bernoulli predictions the logistic Jensen gap is the Bhattacharyya distance between the two predicted distributions. This identity is an instance of the exponential-family result of Nielsen and Boltz. These results supply the mathematical ingredients for measuring what a rotated prediction is worth. [Lyle et al., 2020, Proposition 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00178) · [Wood et al., JMLR, 2023, Theorem 5 and the cross-entropy example](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume24/23-0041/23-0041.pdf) · [Nielsen and Boltz, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2011.2159046)

**Conditional inference.** Kim et al. train an auxiliary network to rank candidate transformations for each test input by their predicted loss. Mocerino et al.'s AdapTTA changes the number of views for each image on an embedded processor. It stops evaluating transformations when the margin between the two top averaged class probabilities passes a threshold. Jitkrittum et al. derive an optimal two-classifier deferral rule for classification error: call the expensive classifier when its conditional increase in correctness justifies the added cost. Predictive confidence provides one practical comparator for a policy based on the expected improvement in loss. [Kim, Kim, and Kim, NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/2ba596643cbbbc20318224181fa46b28-Abstract.html) · [Mocerino et al., IFIP/IEEE VLSI-SoC 2021, Section III](https://doi.org/10.1109/VLSI-SoC53125.2021.9606980) · [Jitkrittum et al., NeurIPS 2023, Proposition 3.1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/1f09e1ee5035a4c3fe38a5681cae5815-Paper-Conference.pdf)

**Course-project contribution.** Given a fixed network and a budget for extra views, which rings inputs benefit most from one additional rotated prediction? Under the symmetry of our data, the mean log-loss improvement in any region preserved by a half-turn equals the mean of the two views' label-free logistic Jensen gap in that region. We estimate this gain on unlabeled inputs before deployment, using either radius alone or radius and angle modulo π. If the regional means were known exactly, selecting the highest-scoring inputs would maximize expected gain among policies observing the same regions. We connect the gains to the geometry learned by GD and pullback, compare both rules with first-view confidence on independent inputs, and explain differences using an orientation-specific loss term. This specialization and its empirical evaluation build on published results on averaging views, measuring disagreement, and allocating inference cost.
