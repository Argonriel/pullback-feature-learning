# Primary-source calibration context

Guo, Pleiss, Sun, and Weinberger (ICML 2017), [On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html), established the practical use of a single positive temperature fitted on held-out data. Their empirical study also connects calibration to training choices including weight decay. This branch uses that standard method and investigates whether correcting the previously observed overconfidence changes the value of selective symmetry averaging. It makes no novelty claim for temperature scaling. In binary logistic prediction, a positive temperature preserves the sign and hence the unaugmented classification decision.

Chidambaram and Ge (ICLR 2024), [On the Limitations of Temperature Scaling for Distributions with Overlaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00740), analyze settings where a scalar post-hoc transformation cannot adequately repair empirical-risk-minimizer calibration under overlapping class distributions. Their particular asymptotic multiclass result is not asserted for this small binary task. The relevant design lesson is narrower: temperature calibration should be tested rather than assumed to solve generalization or class-decision errors, and training changes deserve a separate controlled comparison.

The literature review preceded the new fitting runs. All validation and calibration objectives use sampled labels. A known posterior may give precise evaluation metrics in a synthetic experiment, but using it to fit temperature would confer information unavailable in ordinary deployment; that is explicitly excluded here.
