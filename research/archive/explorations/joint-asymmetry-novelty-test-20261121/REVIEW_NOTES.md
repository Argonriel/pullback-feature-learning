# Consolidated independent-review record

Date: 21 November 2026.

Three separate reviews examined prior art, mathematical reduction, and possible replacements. All support retiring simultaneous marginal and posterior asymmetry as the main innovation. The detailed derivation, primary-source links, and numerical results are in REPORT.md; this record preserves the qualifications needed when reusing that conclusion.

## Mathematical scope

1. The convention here is TV(p,q)=one half of the L1 distance. A paper using the full L1 distance needs a factor-of-two conversion.
2. The displayed support formula allows the two posteriors to vary freely subject to their TV bound. The common-mass derivation attains both extrema, including the boundary cases r=0, r=plus or minus one, and epsilon=0 or one.
3. With a fixed symmetrized input law, a fixed pointwise even-score quota policy, and a genuinely orbitwise rectangular uncertainty class, global sharpness can follow directly by separability. That elementary extension does not establish a new contribution.
4. Shared posterior cells, shared coefficients, or other cross-orbit constraints can prevent simultaneous attainment of pointwise extrema. The archived two-orbit numerical example proves a strict gap for shared posteriors; it does not invalidate the lower bound.
5. A batch quota alone does not supply the required invariance. For the conditional identity, each record's selection mask must remain unchanged when that record is transformed, with the other records fixed. Dependence on the remaining records is allowed under the independent-sampling setup.
6. A fixed-input shared-posterior problem may be a linear program. Jointly unknown input coefficients and posteriors can introduce bilinear parameterizations; no claim is made that every globally constrained formulation is an LP.

## Evidence and priority limits

The novelty rejection uses both an established joint-distribution formulation and an explicit elementary reduction. It does not assert an exact printed match for the project's formula, batch construction, or unbounded log-loss statement. The numerical checks confirm calculations, not originality. The verifier was reviewed independently; no mathematical defect was identified. Floating-point vertex enumeration complements the proof and is not interval-certified optimization.

## Replacement screen

The selected provisional lead is persistence and breakdown of readout/unused-feature decoupling over nonlinear training trajectories. The prior project already has the local derivative of predicted task improvement. Its matrix-operator equivalent is also elementary. Neither is counted as a new result.

An exact persistence example can be constructed in the original two-block residual architecture using structured weights and reflection-paired training records. It is a standard invariant-subspace example; the archived random initializations and ordinary samples do not meet its assumptions. It therefore does not explain the observed approximately 49-fold change in unused-coordinate path length. The unresolved target is a mechanism applicable to actual trajectories, with a falsifiable breakdown prediction beyond a learning-rate or clock effect.

Two broader alternatives were not promoted: generic routing from a cheap score reduces to conditional decision theory, and generic stale-curvature refresh has direct prior-art competition. A restricted selector would need an explicit information or computational access model; expensive evaluation alone is not missing information when the full input and model are already known. A stale positive-definite metric also remains a first-order descent direction, so a finite-step failure must not be described as losing that property.

The replacement remains unvalidated as an innovation. No training was launched, no archived data were rescored, and no historical result was overwritten.
