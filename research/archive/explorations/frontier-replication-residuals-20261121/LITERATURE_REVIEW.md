# Primary-source context

- Cawley and Talbot (2010), [On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html). Selecting a recipe and evaluating its performance on the same noisy outcomes can bias the comparison. Our development/confirmation separation addresses that issue for this particular fixed grid, without claiming the selected allocation is globally optimal.
- Yu and Grauman (2014), [UT Zappos50K official project](https://vision.cs.utexas.edu/projects/finegrained/utzap50k/). The official source documents catalog shoes, categories, metadata and academic/noncommercial terms. Its product identities support a distinct product-group sampling boundary. This is a shifted three-category test, not a full Fashion-MNIST replacement.
- Chen, Dobriban and Lee (2020), [A Group-Theoretic Framework for Data Augmentation](https://jmlr.org/papers/v21/20-163.html). Orbit averaging and approximate invariance have an established theoretical literature. The new work specializes a fixed predictor and invariant quota selector with uncertain orbit density imbalance.
- Maurer and Pontil (2009), [Empirical Bernstein Bounds and Sample Variance Penalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3740). Established concentration inequalities supply finite-sample constants; these are not new methods in this project.
- Ben-David, Lu, Luu and Pal (2010), [Impossibility Theorems for Domain Adaptation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/david10a.html). Assumptions linking inputs and labels are substantive. Finite unlabeled evidence does not establish an unrestricted posterior-symmetry condition.

These sources were checked prospectively on 21 November 2026. Branch notes give additional primary references and exact formulas. The contribution claimed here is the controlled, reproducible follow-up and its specialized derivations, not invention of model selection, distillation, group averaging or concentration.
