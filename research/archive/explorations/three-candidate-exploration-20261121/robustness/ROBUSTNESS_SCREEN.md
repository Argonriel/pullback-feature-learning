# Quantitative robustness screen — not retained as a candidate

Date: 2026-11-21. This is a fresh screen; no old benchmark or retired proposal was rerun.

## Decision

The approximate-classification lane has not produced an independent, defensible innovation candidate. Generic fixed-dimensional distance-to-zero-set bounds, perturbation continuity, and conditioning-dependent trajectory bounds would be standard applications of existing tools. A boundary-layer effect was found in an exact two-dimensional variational calculation, but the baseline dynamics are an explicitly studied singular Riemannian barrier system. It is not counted among the surviving directions.

## What was screened

The exact classification already established in the preceding round is treated as input: for a subspace S of real symmetric matrices, the cubic and quartic closure conditions identify associator ideals in the unital Jordan algebra generated by S. The screen sought something quantitatively stronger than applying Gronwall or a finite-dimensional algebraic error bound to this classification.

1. A fixed-dimension Hölder bound from polynomial closure defects to the exact zero set is a routine real-algebraic direction unless the exponent, constants, or dimension dependence are genuinely sharpened. No such sharp theorem was established here.
2. Dimension-independent rounding of nearly commuting/closed matrix spaces has substantial prior obstacles. Cerjan–Loring's original 2024 paper discusses real-symmetric versus complex-Hermitian distinctions and high-dimensional topological obstructions. This does not prove a result about our precise Jordan criterion, but it rules out treating the general near-commuting problem as an empty field. No topology claim is advanced.
3. A simple cubic scaling construction near a commuting space would largely repackage commutator homogeneity; it was not promoted.
4. A nonuniform small-damping boundary effect was tested in the smallest exact variational system below. It is interesting as a limitation of uniform estimates, but presently has an especially strong classical barrier-method reduction.

## Minimal calculation

Let A1=E11 and A2=E22+theta*(E12+E21). The measured targets are (1,1), X0=0.1 I, and the fixed loss weight W is positive definite. At theta=0 the trajectory is diagonal X=diag(x,y). Set q=W((x,y)-(1,1)), and let z be the derivative of X12 with respect to theta at zero. For the damped full-representation product flow, exact differentiation gives

    d(log x)/dt = -2 q1/(2 x+lambda)
    d(log y)/dt = -2 q2/(2 y+lambda)
    dz/dt = -(x+y)/(x+y+lambda) q2
            + lambda/(2(x+y+lambda)) (d(log x)/dt+d(log y)/dt) z.

Initial z is zero. The implementation uses adaptive RK4 with step doubling in log-diagonal coordinates; it is a deterministic ODE check, not model training. It uses NumPy only. The output records endpoint residuals and integrator steps.

For W=I, z at the interpolating endpoint tends toward 0.9 as lambda decreases. A more oblique W with eigenvalues 1 and 100,

    [[15.4982143313, -35.0017856687],
     [-35.0017856687, 85.5017856687]],

produces the following values:

| lambda | Endpoint z | Minimum log diagonal | Endpoint measurement residual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1 | 0.986197811 | -4.28065 | 4.11e-13 |
| 0.03 | 0.928883247 | -6.48249 | 8.52e-13 |
| 0.01 | 0.911852816 | -13.20062 | 9.00e-14 |
| 0.003 | 0.905880364 | -37.15162 | 1.91e-13 |
| 0.001 | 0.904172484 | -105.58380 | 1.91e-12 |

These observations suggest a singular boundary layer as lambda approaches zero. They do not establish a limiting endpoint derivative, a noncommutation of limits, or a finite-theta theorem. No extrapolated constant is claimed as proved. The regularity of the theta derivative is only being used at each fixed positive lambda.

## Strong primary overlap

[Attouch, Bolte, Redont, and Teboulle, *Singular Riemannian Barrier Methods and Gradient-Projection Dynamical Systems for Constrained Optimization* (2004)](https://bolte.perso.math.cnrs.fr/SingularRiemannianBarrier.pdf), Section 2 and Theorem 2.1, study the regularized Lotka–Volterra equation xdot_j=-x_j/(mu+nu*x_j) partial_j Phi. Our diagonal baseline is exactly this equation with nu=1 and mu=lambda/2. Their Figure 1 even uses a two-dimensional quadratic with an interior minimizer and trajectories that approach the boundary as the barrier decreases. They identify the limiting projected-dual system, which is generally different from the usual tangent-cone projected gradient flow. This is a direct reduction of the baseline, not just an analogy. The transverse response above is not stated there, but has not been shown to contain a sufficiently independent contribution.

[Cerjan and Loring, *Even spheres as joint spectra of matrix models* (2024)](https://alexcerjan.com/documents/papers/loring_even_spheres_jmaa_2024.pdf), Section 7, was inspected for the nearly commuting obstruction. Its matrix-field and dimension qualifications must be preserved; complex results cannot automatically be asserted for real symmetric triples.
