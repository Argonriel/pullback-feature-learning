# Boundary and inconsistent-data extension: independent proof audit

Date: 2026-11-21 (America/Los_Angeles). Status: complete mathematical draft; no priority claim. This extends the previously archived proof rather than introducing a new general convergence mechanism.

## Exact statement and quantifiers

Let `n,m` be finite positive integers, let `A: Sym(n) -> R^m` be linear, and put `S=range(A*)`. Let `J=Jordan(S,I)` be the unital real matrix Jordan algebra generated by `S`, and assume

    N_J := J intersect S-perp  subset center(J),

where centrality means ordinary matrix commutation with every element of `J`. For every `lambda>0`, every fixed symmetric `W>0` on `R^m`, every `y in R^m`, and every `X0 in J` with `X0>0`, define

    f(X)=1/2 (A X-y)^T W (A X-y),
    G(X)=A* W(A X-y),
    K_X(U)=XU+UX,
    Xdot=-K_X(K_X+lambda Id)^(-1)G(X),  X(0)=X0.

There is a unique global solution, it remains positive definite for every finite time, and it converges to a matrix `X*>=0`. Define the ambient closed-cone optimal set

    C = argmin { f(Z): Z in Sym(n), Z>=0 }.

Then:

1. `C` is nonempty and `X* in C`.
2. Every `Z in C` satisfies `ker(X*) subset ker(Z)`. Equivalently, `X*` belongs to the relative interior of the smallest face of the PSD cone containing `C`; its support is the maximal support in `C`.
3. Extend `s log s` continuously by `0 log 0=0` and set

       Phi_lambda(Z)=1/2 ||Z||_F^2 + (lambda/2) tr(Z log Z-Z),  Z>=0.

   With the anchor `X0>0`, the Bregman objective is finite on the entire PSD cone:

       D_Phi(Z,X0)=Phi(Z)-Phi(X0)-<X0+(lambda/2)log X0,Z-X0>.

   The limit is its unique ambient minimizer over `C`:

       X* = argmin_{Z in C} D_Phi(Z,X0).

4. `X*>0` if and only if `C` contains a positive definite matrix.
5. If a PSD exact interpolant exists, `C={Z>=0:A Z=y}`. The endpoint then does not depend on `W`. If data are inconsistent on the PSD cone, `C` can depend on `W`, and so can the endpoint.

No strict feasibility or interpolation assumption is used in items 1--3. The theorem does not cover zero damping, singular starts, incompatible starts outside `J`, or discrete iterations. A general positive spectral lower bound or exponential rate is not asserted at a singular endpoint.

## 1. Jordan projection and conservation

The orthogonal projection `P_J` is positive and unital. Indeed a negative spectral projector of `P_J Z` belongs to `J`; pairing it with `Z>=0` rules out a negative eigenvalue. Applying positivity to `Z-aI` also preserves any lower bound `Z>=aI`. Since `S subset J`, `A P_J Z=A Z`. Thus `P_J` maps ambient PSD minimizers into PSD minimizers in `J` whenever such minimizers exist.

The operators `K_X` and their positive resolvents preserve `J` when `X in J` is positive definite. The trajectory consequently stays in `J` while it remains positive definite. For every `N in N_J`, centrality and self-adjointness give

    d/dt <N, X+(lambda/2)log X>
      = <N,(Id+lambda K_X^(-1)) Xdot>
      = -<N,G(X)> = 0.                                      (1)

The same conservation calculation was already established in the earlier archive. No full Hessian representation is used.

## 2. Boundedness does not need feasibility

Loss decrease implies bounded `A X(t)`. Since `A` is injective on `S`, it implies bounded `P_S X(t)`.

Suppose a sequence on the maximal interval had `r_k=||X_k||_F -> infinity`. Taking a subsequence gives `X_k/r_k -> V>=0`, `||V||_F=1`, and `V in N_J`. In particular `V` commutes with every `X_k`. On `ran(V)`, let `nu` be the smallest positive eigenvalue of `V`; eventually

    X_k|ran(V) >= (r_k nu/2) I.

The kernel of `V` contributes nothing to the trace pairing, so

    <V,log X_k> >= tr(V) log(r_k nu/2) -> infinity,
    <V,X_k> / r_k -> ||V||_F^2=1.

This contradicts conservation (1). Products are therefore bounded before any convergence or optimality assertion. This streamlined argument only uses centrality, not an explicit simple-factor decomposition.

## 3. Global existence and finite-length convergence

Choose a square invertible factor `F0` with `F0 F0^T=X0` and solve

    Fdot=-H F,
    H=(K_(F F^T)+lambda Id)^(-1)G(F F^T).

The factor vector field is real analytic everywhere because the operator being inverted, `K_(F F^T)+lambda Id`, has eigenvalues at least `lambda`; its inverse has norm at most `1/lambda`. Its product solves the stated equation. Bounded products imply bounded factors, hence global existence. The fundamental matrix equation preserves invertibility at every finite time.

For `L(F)=f(F F^T)` and a global product bound `||X||op<=R`, the previously checked estimates are

    -Ldot >= ||grad L||_F^2 / [2(2R+lambda)],
    ||Fdot||_F <= ||grad L||_F / (2 lambda).                  (2)

For clarity, the standard analytic argument is short. On a bounded trajectory, the omega limit set is compact and has a common limiting objective value `ell`. The descent estimate makes `||grad L||^2` integrable. Compactness and smoothness of the factor vector field make `grad L(F(t))` uniformly continuous, so it tends to zero and the omega limit set consists of critical points. A finite cover by analytic Lojasiewicz neighborhoods supplies constants `C>0`, `theta in (0,1/2]` with

    (L-ell)^(1-theta) <= C ||grad L||

eventually. Combining this with (2) bounds the remaining path length by a constant times `(L-ell)^theta`. Hence `F(t)` converges, and so do `X(t)` and `H(t)`. This is classical analytic descent theory, not an innovation claim.

## 4. Convex PSD optimality without consistent data

Suppose the limiting symmetric generator `H*` had a negative eigenvalue. Put `A(t)=-H(t)` and decompose the space orthogonally into the positive spectral subspace `E_u` of `A*=-H*` and its complement `E_s`, on which `A*` is nonpositive. Let `a>0` be the smallest eigenvalue on `E_u`. Choose `0<epsilon<a/4` and `T` such that `||A(t)-A*||op<=epsilon` for `t>=T`.

Write a solution of `ydot=A(t)y` as `y=u+s` in this decomposition. On the boundary of the cone `||s||<=||u||`, symmetry cancels the two off-diagonal pairings in the derivative and gives

    (1/2) d/dt (||s||^2-||u||^2)
       <= epsilon ||s||^2-(a-epsilon)||u||^2
       = -(a-2 epsilon)||u||^2 <0.

Thus the cone is forward invariant. Inside it,

    (1/2) d/dt ||u||^2
       >= (a-epsilon)||u||^2-epsilon ||u|| ||s||
       >= (a-2 epsilon)||u||^2.

Launch the solution with nonzero `u(T)` and `s(T)=0`. Its unstable component grows at least as `exp((a-2 epsilon)(t-T))`. But invertibility of `F(T)` represents the same solution as

    y(t)=F(t) F(T)^(-1)y(T),

which is bounded because `F(t)` is bounded and the coefficient vector is fixed. This contradiction proves `H*>=0` without discarding any exceptional initial factors.

Convergence in the autonomous factor equation implies `H* F*=0`, and therefore

    H*X*=X*H*=0,
    G*=(K_(X*)+lambda Id)H*=lambda H*>=0,
    <G*,X*>=0.

For every `Z>=0`, convexity gives

    f(Z)>=f(X*)+<G*,Z-X*> = f(X*)+<G*,Z> >= f(X*).

Thus `X*` is a global PSD minimizer, proving nonemptiness of `C` as a conclusion. This step never needed an exact interpolant. Because the measurement loss is strictly convex in `A Z`, any two elements of `C` have identical measurements: otherwise their midpoint has smaller loss.

## 5. The central boundary lemma

**Lemma.** Suppose `X(t)>0` lies in `J`, `X(t)->X*>=0`, and (1) holds. Let `N in N_J` satisfy `X*+N>=0`. Then `N` annihilates `ker(X*)`. Moreover, with `P` the support projector of `X*`,

    lim <N,log X(t)> = <N,log_P X*>,                       (3)

where `log_P X*` is the logarithm on `ran(P)`, extended by zero on its kernel.

**Proof.** Decompose into eigenspaces of `N`. Since `N` is central, each such eigenspace is invariant for every `X(t)` and for `X*`. On an eigenspace of eigenvalue `a<0`, the condition `X*+N>=0` implies `X*>=-a I`, so its logarithm has a finite limit. On an eigenspace of eigenvalue `a>0`, a zero eigenvalue of `X*` would make `a tr log X(t)` tend to negative infinity. All other singular contributions have nonnegative coefficients, while negative-coefficient contributions stay finite. The bounded linear term cannot cancel this divergence in (1). Hence `X*` is positive definite on every nonzero eigenspace of `N`. It follows both that `N ker(X*)=0` and that (3) holds. QED.

For any `Z in C`, let `Z_J=P_J Z`. Positivity and equal measurements give `Z_J in C` and `N=Z_J-X* in N_J`. Apply the lemma to obtain `N ker(X*)=0`.

Let `E=I-P` be the kernel projector of `X*`; it belongs to `J` by spectral calculus. Then

    tr(E Z)=tr(E Z_J)=tr(E X*)+tr(E N)=0.

Since `Z>=0`, this forces `Z E=0`. Thus every ambient minimizer belongs to the face supported on `P`. In the simple-factor decomposition, the stronger description is that every projected minimizer agrees with `X*` on each singular simple factor, because a central scalar difference that annihilates one kernel direction is zero on the factor.

## 6. Ambient Bregman optimality on the common face

Let `A0=grad Phi(X0)=X0+(lambda/2)log X0`. On the face `Z=P Z P>=0`, the objective `Phi(Z)-<A0,Z>` is differentiable at `X*`, because `X*` is positive definite relative to that face. Its derivative, embedded in the ambient space, is

    B = X*+(lambda/2)log_P X* - P A0 P.

This matrix belongs to `J`. For the last term, use the Jordan quadratic representation

    P A0 P = 2 P circ(P circ A0)-P^2 circ A0.

For every ambient `Z in C`, take `N=P_J Z-X*` as above. Since `N=P N P`, conservation (1), convergence (3), and orthogonality of `P_J` give

    <B,Z-X*> = <B,N>
             = <N, X*+(lambda/2)log_P X*-A0>
             = 0.

The first-order convex inequality on this face proves `D_Phi(Z,X0)>=D_Phi(X*,X0)`. The quadratic term makes the objective strictly (indeed 1-strongly) convex, so equality holds only for `Z=X*`. This proves the *ambient* characterization without asserting that a singular matrix has an ordinary ambient logarithmic gradient, and without assuming an unproved Jensen inequality for `P_J`.

## 7. Exact necessity and limitations

If `C` contains an SPD matrix, the common-kernel conclusion forces `ker(X*)={0}`. Conversely an SPD limit is itself an SPD member of `C`. Therefore strict positive definiteness of an optimal solution is exactly equivalent to a strictly positive definite limit under these hypotheses. For consistent targets, strict feasibility is necessary and sufficient for an SPD endpoint, but unnecessary for global convergence or the closed-PSD Bregman selection law.

The scalar counterexample in `EXACT_CONTROLS.md` shows that a boundary endpoint can decay only polynomially. The zero-damping and singular-start counterexamples there show that these two assumptions cannot simply be dropped. Inconsistent data retain the minimizer-set formulation but generally lose independence from the observation weight.

## Prior-work boundary and disposition

[Alvarez, Bolte, and Brahic (2004)](https://isr.umd.edu/Labs/ISL/HELMKE/AlBra_SICON04.pdf), Theorem 4.7, already treats boundary convergence for convex Hessian flows. Corollary 4.8 identifies a Bregman projection for linear objectives; Proposition 4.11 treats active coordinates in linear programming. These are close precedents for the common-face argument. The present mobility is generally not a Hessian metric on noncommuting matrix blocks, so those statements do not directly supply this theorem. Boundary convergence, entropy support selection, and Bregman minimization are not new general principles.

**Disposition: retain as a scoped extension of the existing candidate, not a separate innovation.** The technically useful addition is that the same classified non-Hessian matrix flow admits a closed-cone endpoint theorem for arbitrary data, with a rigorous ambient face argument. Its novelty and significance remain subject to the root report's broader primary-source audit. Failure to find a matching source would not establish priority.
